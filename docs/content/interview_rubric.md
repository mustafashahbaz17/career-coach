# Interview answer rubric

Used by the interview simulator to grade a student's answer. Each answer is scored on 4 criteria from 1 to 5. The total is out of 20.

## 1. Structure

Does the answer have a clear shape that is easy to follow (for example STAR: Situation, Task, Action, Result)?

| Score | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 1 | Rambling or jumbled, with no clear start, middle or end. |
| 3 | Some order, but a part is missing (often the result) or the listener has to work to follow it. |
| 5 | Clear opening, a logical flow and a result or conclusion, in about one to two minutes. |

## 2. Relevance

Does the answer actually respond to the question that was asked?

| Score | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 1 | Mostly off-topic, or a memorised answer to a different question. |
| 3 | Partly answers the question but drifts, or only covers one half of it. |
| 5 | Answers exactly what was asked, and the example chosen fits the question and the role. |

## 3. Specific evidence

Does the student back up what they say with real, concrete details?

| Score | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 1 | Only general claims such as "I am hardworking" with no example. |
| 3 | One example, but vague: no numbers, names of actions or clear outcome. |
| 5 | A real example with specific actions the student took themselves and a measurable or clearly stated result. |

## 4. Clear communication

Is the answer easy to understand, confident and appropriate in tone?

| Score | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 1 | Hard to understand: very short, very long, or full of filler and jargon. |
| 3 | Understandable, but with some filler, unclear phrases or flat delivery. |
| 5 | Plain, confident and concise, with the right tone for an interview. |

## Total and bands

| Total (out of 20) | Band | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 17 to 20 | Strong | Ready to use in a real interview. |
| 11 to 16 | Average | Good base; one or two criteria need work. |
| 4 to 10 | Weak | Needs a rewrite before using in an interview. |

## Grading rules

- Score each criterion on its own. A student can score 5 on communication and 1 on evidence.
- Grade only what the student wrote or said. Do not reward or punish name, gender, accent, university or background.
- Do not invent details the student did not give. If the example is missing, score evidence low.
- Always give feedback: one thing done well, then one specific thing to improve, for each criterion.
- Feedback is advice, not a hiring decision.
