CV critique rubric

Used by the CV studio to review a student's CV. Each CV is scored on 6 criteria. For each one, this page says what good looks like and the 3 most common fixes. Suggestions are drafts for the student to accept, edit or reject. The student always decides.

1. Impact verbs and results

What good looks like: every bullet starts with a strong action verb (led, organised, built, analysed, raised, reduced) and says what changed because of it.

Most common fixes

Replace weak openers ("Responsible for", "Helped with", "Worked on") with an action verb.
Add the result: what improved, how much, or who benefited.
Describe what you did, not what the team did in general.
2. Numbers

What good looks like: most experience bullets contain at least one real number (people, rupees, percent, time, items).

Most common fixes

Add a figure the student already knows: team size, audience size, event attendance, money raised or saved.
Use ranges or "about" only if the exact number is unknown, and never invent a figure.
Put the number near the start of the bullet so it is easy to spot.
3. Relevance to the target role

What good looks like: the strongest and most relevant experience, skills and projects come first, and the wording matches the role the student is applying for.

Most common fixes

Move the most relevant experience, project or society role higher.
Reuse key words from the job advert where the student really has that skill.
Cut or shorten items that do not help for this role.
4. Structure and length

What good looks like: one page, in a logical order (contact, education, experience, leadership and societies, projects, skills, achievements), with the latest items first.

Most common fixes

Cut to one page: drop old school results, repeat bullets and filler.
Use reverse chronological order inside each section.
Keep 2 to 4 bullets per role, each one or two lines long.
5. Formatting and ATS readability

What good looks like: a clean single-column layout with standard section headings, one simple font, consistent dates and spacing, and no text inside images. It exports to a PDF with real, selectable text.

Most common fixes

Remove tables, text boxes, columns and images that hide text from software.
Use standard headings such as Education, Experience and Skills.
Make dates, bullets, bold and spacing consistent throughout.
6. Spelling and consistency

What good looks like: no spelling or grammar mistakes, the same tense and style throughout, and names of organisations and tools spelled correctly.

Most common fixes

Run a spell check and read the CV aloud once.
Use past tense for finished roles and present tense only for current ones.
Write dates, CGPA and organisation names in the same way everywhere.
Scoring

Score each criterion from 1 to 5, where 1 means the problem is severe, 3 means it is acceptable but can improve, and 5 means it is strong. Give feedback for each criterion in the same order as above.

Rules for the reviewer
Never invent experience, skills, grades or numbers. Only suggest changes based on what is already in the CV, or ask the student a question.
Do not change the student's grades, dates or qualifications.
Do not judge by gender, nationality, religion, age, name, photo or university.
Suggest, do not decide. Every change is a draft for the student to accept, edit or reject.
Be kind and specific. Say one thing that is working before the fixes.
