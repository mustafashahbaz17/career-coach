# What to wear to an interview

There is no single rule. Companies differ, and what suits one office can feel wrong in another. Use this as a starting point, and when in doubt, **ask**. It is completely normal to email the recruiter: "Is there a dress code for the interview?"

**The goal:** look neat, comfortable and appropriate, so the interviewer remembers your answers, not your outfit.

## By setting

### Corporate, banking and finance
- **Aim for:** formal and conservative.
- **Western formal:** a suit or blazer with a shirt or blouse and formal trousers or skirt, in navy, grey, black or white.
- **Shalwar kameez:** a plain, well-fitted shalwar kameez in a solid colour (white, cream, grey, navy or black), often with a waistcoat or formal jacket, and formal shoes. This is widely accepted in Pakistani offices.
- **Avoid:** loud prints, heavy embroidery and very casual shoes.

### FMCG, marketing and consumer brands
- **Aim for:** smart and a little more personality than banking.
- **Western:** a blazer with smart trousers, a shirt, a dress or a skirt of suitable length.
- **Shalwar kameez:** a neat kurta or kameez with trousers or shalwar, in clean colours with simple details.
- **Tip:** one small touch of colour or style is fine. It should look professional, not like a party.

### Tech and startups
- **Aim for:** smart casual. Many teams dress casually every day.
- **Wear:** a clean shirt or polo, a neat kurta, or a plain top, with chinos, trousers or neat jeans.
- **Avoid:** overdressing in a heavy suit unless they said so, and also avoid torn or very worn clothes.
- **Tip:** if you can't tell, go one step smarter than the team's likely dress.

### NGOs, development sector and government
- **Aim for:** modest, neat and respectful.
- **Wear:** a plain shalwar kameez or kurta, or smart western clothes, with sensible shoes. Many offices expect a modest style, with a dupatta or shawl if that is your practice.
- **Avoid:** flashy clothes or anything that could look careless.

### Video interviews
- **Dress fully,** not only the top half, in case you stand up.
- **Colours:** solid, medium tones look best on camera. Avoid very bright white, tight stripes and busy patterns, which can look odd on screen.
- **Camera and background:** sit facing a window or a lamp, with a tidy and plain background behind you. Camera at eye level.
- **Check:** your sound, internet and charger 15 minutes before. Close other apps and silence your phone.

## Practical tips for every interview

- **Iron your clothes** the night before and check for stains, loose buttons and loose threads.
- **Keep it simple.** Plain is better than clever.
- **Comfortable shoes.** Clean and polished, and shoes you can walk in, because you may walk, wait and stand.
- **Plan for the weather.** Heat, rain and cold are all common, so carry an umbrella or shawl and keep your outfit neat when you arrive.
- **Light fragrance only,** or none.
- **Hair and nails** neat and tidy.
- **Bag:** a simple folder or bag with printed CV copies, a pen, your ID and a notebook.
- **Arrive early,** with time to sit, breathe and check your outfit in a mirror.
- **Wear it before the day.** Try the full outfit once, so you know it fits and feels comfortable when you sit.

## If you don't own formal clothes

- **Borrow** from a friend, sibling or relative. This is very common and nobody minds.
- **Check** with your university career office. Some run clothing swaps or lend items for interviews.
- **Simple is enough.** A clean, ironed, plain shirt or kurta in a solid colour with neat trousers works in most settings.

## Remember

Clothes can help you feel confident, but they are a small part of an interview. Interviewers mostly remember how you answered, how you listened and whether you were prepared. A neat, comfortable outfit lets you focus on that.

*This is general guidance, not an official rule. Always follow any instructions given by the company.*
